The classic red, glossy, slightly-sweet-and-very-spicy rice cake dish sold from
plastic pojangmacha carts all over Seoul. Made with **store-bought garaetteok**
(frozen or fresh cylindrical rice cakes) — no soaking rice flour, no steaming.

Serves 2 generously as a snack, or 1 hungry person as a meal.

## Ingredients
- 400 g garaetteok (cylindrical Korean rice cakes — fresh or frozen, both work)
- 100 g Korean fish cake (eomuk / 어묵), sliced into 4 cm strips
- 1 spring onion, white part cut into 4 cm lengths, green finely sliced (separate)
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 200 ml water (or anchovy-kelp broth if you have it — much better)
- 1.5 tbsp gochujang (Korean red pepper paste)
- 1 tbsp gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes) — adjust to taste
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp sugar (or Korean rice syrup, jocheong, if you have it)
- 1 tsp minced garlic (extra, optional)
- 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (to finish)
- 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (to finish)

## Method
1. **Prep the rice cakes.** If frozen, soak in cold water for 20–30 minutes until pliable (a bent cake shouldn't snap). If fresh, just give them 10 minutes in cool water. Slice any extra-thick ones lengthwise so they cook evenly and the sauce clings.
2. **Boil the rice cakes first, separately.** Bring a pot of water to a boil, drop the rice cakes in, and cook 2–3 minutes until they float and are tender all the way through (bite one — no chalky center). Drain. This step matters: it guarantees they're cooked through before they hit the sauce, since the sauce only simmers briefly.
3. **Make the sauce.** In a wide pan (not a wok — you want surface area, not depth), combine the water, gochujang, gochugaru, soy sauce, sugar, and garlic. Whisk until smooth. Bring to a boil over medium heat, stirring.
4. **Add the rice cakes and fish cake.** Lower heat to medium-low. Simmer 5–7 minutes, stirring often, until the sauce reduces to a glossy, sticky coating that clings to the cakes. The sauce should look like a thick red glaze, not a soup.
5. **Add the spring onion whites** in the last 2 minutes so they stay snappy.
6. **Finish.** Off heat: stir in the sesame oil. Top with the sliced green tops of the spring onion and a pinch of sesame seeds. Serve immediately, in the pan or piled into a bowl. Eat with toothpicks or chopsticks — the sauce is lava-hot.

## Notes
- **Don't skip the pre-boil.** Tteokbokki fails when the rice cakes are still chalky in the middle because they didn't get cooked through. The 2–3 minute boil in plain water is the insurance policy.
- **Gochujang is salty and gochugaru is not** — they're not interchangeable. You need both for the classic flavor (sweet-salt-spice). If you must pick one, use gochujang.
- **Anchovy-kelp broth (myeolchi-yukhoe broth)** elevates this from "good" to "why does this taste like a restaurant." Simmer 5 g dried anchovies + a 10 cm piece of kombu in 500 ml water for 10 minutes, then strain. Use that instead of the plain water in step 3.
- **Cheese tteokbokki** is a modern variation but not the classic — add a slice of mozzarella on top in the last minute if you want to try it.
- **Leftover sauce is gold.** Stir it into fried rice, use it to glaze spam, or spoon it over a soft-boiled egg on rice the next morning. Nothing wasted.
- **No fish cake?** Skip it. The dish is still tteokbokki.
