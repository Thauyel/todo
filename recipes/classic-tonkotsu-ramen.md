The bowl that started a citywide religion in Fukuoka. Cloudy pork-bone broth,
shoyu tare, straight alkaline noodles, a marinated soft egg, and a fistful of
fresh aromatics on top.

Serves 4 generously. Plan a weekend — the broth alone takes 8–12 hours, but
it scales and freezes perfectly.

## Broth (tonkotsu)
- 1.5 kg pork bones (a mix of femur + trotters gives the best body)
- 500 g pork belly or shoulder (the protein; becomes your chashu)
- 2 large onions, halved (skin on — colour)
- 1 whole head of garlic, halved across the equator
- 50 g fresh ginger, smashed
- 4 spring onions, white parts only, lightly crushed
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- Cold water to cover (about 4 L)

## Tare (shoyu)
- 120 ml soy sauce (good Japanese shoyu — Kikkoman or Yugeta)
- 2 tbsp mirin
- 1 tbsp sake
- 1 tsp rice vinegar
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 1 small garlic clove, grated

## Noodles & Toppings
- 4 portions fresh alkaline ramen noodles (120 g dried, or 180 g fresh per bowl)
- 4 marinated soft-boiled eggs (ajitsuke tamago)
- 200 g bamboo shoots (menma), rinsed
- 2 spring onions, finely sliced (greens only)
- 1 sheet nori, halved
- 1 tbsp white pepper or freshly ground toasted sesame (to finish)
- 1 tsp lard or schmaltz (optional — the Fukuoka secret)

## Method — broth (day 1, evening)
1. Blanch the bones: cover with cold water, bring to a boil, simmer 10 minutes. Drain and rinse every bone and piece of meat under cold running water until the water runs clear. This is non-negotiable — it removes the scum and bitter blood.
2. In a heavy stockpot, char the onions, garlic, and ginger directly over a gas flame or in a dry pan until deeply blackened in spots. Set aside.
3. Return bones and meat to the pot. Add cold water to cover (about 4 L). Bring slowly to a bare simmer over 45 minutes — you want the water to reach ~95 °C, not a rolling boil, before you crank it.
4. Once at temperature, crank the heat to a **rolling boil**. Maintain this hard boil for **8–12 hours**, stirring every 30 minutes, skimming the grey foam off the surface. Add water to keep the bones submerged. The emulsion you're building — fat + collagen + water — is what makes tonkotsu tonkotsu. Do not stop stirring.
5. After hour 6, add the charred aromatics and the spring onion whites. Continue boiling.
6. At hour 8–12, the broth should be opaque, milky-white, and viscous enough to coat a spoon. Strain through a fine mesh. Cool quickly (ice bath) and refrigerate overnight. Lift off the solidified fat cap in the morning — save it, that's your finishing lard.

## Method — chashu (day 1, while broth boils)
1. Roll a 500 g piece of pork belly into a tight cylinder. Tie with butcher's twine every 3 cm.
2. Sear in a dry pan until caramelised on all sides.
3. Submerge in a mixture of 250 ml soy sauce + 250 ml mirin + 250 ml sake + 100 g sugar + 4 crushed garlic cloves + a thumb of ginger.
4. Simmer 90 minutes, turning every 20. Cool in the liquid, then refrigerate overnight in the same braise.
5. Slice into 5 mm coins just before serving.

## Method — ajitsuke tamago (marinated eggs)
1. Soft-boil 4 eggs: 6 min 30 s from cold, then ice bath. Peel.
2. Submerge in 60 ml shoyu + 60 ml mirin + 1 tsp sugar for at least 4 hours, ideally overnight.

## Method — assembly (day 2)
1. Reheat the broth gently. Whisk 1 tsp of the reserved fat cap back into each portion — this is what gives the glossy mouthfeel.
2. Per bowl: stir 2–3 tbsp tare into the bottom of a warm bowl. Ladle 350–400 ml hot broth over it.
3. Cook noodles separately in unsalted boiling water (the broth is already seasoned) for the time on the package minus 30 seconds. Drain, shake off water.
4. Place noodles in the bowl. Top with 4–5 chashu slices, half a marinated egg (cut with a wet knife so the yolk stays clean), a small handful of menma, a generous pinch of sliced spring onion greens, and a triangle of nori standing up against the side of the bowl.
5. Finish with white pepper or toasted sesame, and a tiny spoon of that fat if you didn't already add it.

## Notes
- **The hard boil is the technique.** Tonkotsu is not a clear broth. If your water isn't violently boiling, the collagen won't emulsify. Use the biggest pot you have and the hottest burner.
- **Skim early, then stop.** The grey foam in the first hour is scum — remove it. After that, the white froth IS your broth. Don't skim it off.
- **No salt in the noodle water.** The tare + chashu braise + egg marinade are all salty. Salted noodle water throws the whole bowl off.
- **Freeze the broth in 400 ml portions.** Up to 3 months. Thaw overnight, reheat gently, whisk in fresh fat, assemble as above. Weeknight ramen is now 15 minutes.
- **The classic Fukuoka bowl is shoyu-tonkotsu** (what's here). If you want it cleaner and saltier, halve the tare. If you want it richer and sweeter (Hakata-style), skip the tare and season with just salt (shio-tonkotsu).
